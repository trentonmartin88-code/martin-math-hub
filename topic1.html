Whole-Number Division

What to Remember

Dividend ÷ Divisor = QuotientIf there is something left that cannot make another complete group, that amount is the remainder.
  • Estimate first so you know what size answer makes sense.
  • Divide one place at a time.
  • Multiply, subtract, then bring down.
  • Check by multiplying quotient × divisor, then add the remainder.

How to Answer It

1Circle what the question is asking: quotient, remainder, or both.
2Estimate the quotient.
3Use long division carefully.
4Check that remainder < divisor and answer the question in context.
Example1,888 ÷ 14 = 134 R12. Check: 134 × 14 + 12 = 1,888.

Decimal Operations

Add & Subtract

Line up decimal points.Add zeros as placeholders when needed. Regroup normally.
Example21.60 − 12.95 − 1.29 − 2.37: work one step at a time and estimate first so your result is reasonable.

Multiply & Divide

Multiply decimalsMultiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal using the total number of decimal places in the factors.
Divide by a decimalMove the divisor's decimal until it becomes a whole number. Move the dividend's decimal the same number of places, then divide.

Divide Fractions

The Procedure

1Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions.
2Keep the first fraction.
3Change ÷ to ×.
4Flip the second fraction to its reciprocal.
5Multiply, simplify, and interpret the quotient.

Make Sense of the Answer

Dividing by a fraction less than 1The quotient is often larger because you are asking how many small groups fit inside the amount.
Example4 ÷ 2/3 = 4 × 3/2 = 6. Six groups of 2/3 fit into 4.

Factors & the Distributive Property

Factor an Expression

Find a common factor shared by both terms, then divide each term by that factor.

Example36 + 27 = 9(4 + 3). Check by distributing: 9×4 + 9×3 = 36 + 27.

How to Check Choices

When the test gives multiple factored expressions, distribute each choice mentally or on scratch paper. The correct choice must rebuild the original expression exactly.

MULTI-STEP WORD PROBLEMS

Do not start calculating just because you see numbers. Read the final question first, decide what each number means, write the operations in order, estimate, solve, and label your answer.

Topic 1 Quick Practice

Try these without the answer key first.

1 • Whole-Number Division

3,745 books are packed equally into 28 boxes. What is the greatest number of books in each box, and how many are left over?

2 • Decimal Addition/Subtraction

A class collected $184.75 Monday and $229.60 Tuesday, then spent $96.85. How much remained?

3 • Decimal Multiplication

A worker earns $15.20 per hour for an 8.5-hour shift. How much does the worker earn?

4 • Decimal Division

18.9 liters are divided equally among 6 containers. How many liters are in each?

5 • Fraction Division

How many 3/4-cup servings can be made from 4 1/2 cups?

6 • Fraction Reasoning

Without calculating first, decide whether 5/6 ÷ 1/4 will be greater than or less than 5/6. Explain.

7 • Factoring

Factor 24 + 36 using the greatest common factor.

8 • Multi-Step

A store has 125.5 pounds of rice. It sells 18 bags weighing 4.25 pounds each. How many pounds remain?

Check Answers & Best First Move
1. 133 R21. First move: estimate 3,745 ÷ 28, then long divide.
2. $317.50. First move: add the two collections, then subtract the expense.
3. $129.20. First move: multiply 15.20 × 8.5 and place the decimal carefully.
4. 3.15 L. First move: 18.9 ÷ 6.
5. 6 servings. First move: 4 1/2 = 9/2, then divide by 3/4.
6. Greater. Dividing by 1/4 asks how many fourths fit in 5/6, so the quotient is larger.
7. 12(2 + 3). First move: find the GCF of 24 and 36.
8. 49 pounds. First move: find the amount sold, 18 × 4.25 = 76.5, then subtract from 125.5.
