Topic 3 • Numerical & Algebraic Expressions

Expressions

Move from exponents and numerical expressions into variables, equivalent expressions, and simplifying algebraic expressions.

Big Ideas

Numbers have structure

Exponents, prime factors, GCF, LCM, and order of operations help us represent and work with numerical expressions efficiently.

Variables represent quantities

Algebraic expressions use letters to describe patterns, unknown values, and real-world relationships.

Properties preserve value

We can evaluate, rewrite, and simplify expressions while using mathematical properties to keep equivalent expressions equal in value.

Lesson 3-1: Understand and Represent Exponents

An exponent is a compact way to represent repeated multiplication of the same factor.

Exponent Vocabulary

Base, exponent, and power

5³ = 5 × 5 × 5The base is 5. The exponent is 3. The exponent tells how many times the base is used as a factor.
A powerAn expression containing a base and an exponent, such as 10⁴ or (2/3)².
Zero exponentAny nonzero number raised to the 0 power has a value of 1.
How to Evaluate

Expand first when needed

1Identify the base.
2Use the exponent to count how many times the base is a factor.
3Multiply the repeated factors.
4If the base is a fraction or decimal, the same rule still applies.
WHOLE NUMBER

4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.

FRACTION

(2/5)² = 2/5 × 2/5 = 4/25.

POWERS OF 10

10⁵ = 100,000. The exponent matches the number of zeros.

ZERO POWER

7⁰ = 1. The base must be nonzero.

Common mistake: multiplying base × exponent

5⁴ does not mean 5 × 4. It means 5 × 5 × 5 × 5.

Lesson 3-2: Greatest Common Factor & Least Common Multiple

Prime factorization helps reveal the structure of numbers and gives an organized way to find GCF and LCM.

Prime Factorization & GCF

Break numbers into prime factors

Prime factorizationWrite a composite number as a product of prime numbers. Example: 24 = 2³ × 3.
Greatest Common FactorMultiply the common prime factors using the lowest power shared by both numbers.
Example24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². GCF = 2² × 3 = 12.
LCM

Find the smallest shared multiple

Least Common MultipleUse every prime factor needed, taking the greatest power that appears in either factorization.
Example12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3². LCM = 2² × 3² = 36.
Word-problem clueGCF often helps make the greatest number of equal groups. LCM often helps determine when repeating events occur together again.
GCF & DISTRIBUTIVE PROPERTY

24 + 36 = 12(2 + 3). Factoring out the GCF rewrites the sum as a product.

LCM IN CONTEXT

One event repeats every 6 days and another every 8 days. They occur together again after LCM(6,8) = 24 days.

GCF and LCM answer different questions

GCF is the greatest factor two numbers share. LCM is the least positive multiple they share. Do not choose based only on seeing the word “common.”

Lesson 3-3: Write and Evaluate Numerical Expressions

Numerical expressions may contain exponents, fractions, decimals, parentheses, brackets, and several operations. Everyone gets the same value by following the same order of operations.

Order of Operations

Grouping → exponents → ×/÷ → +/−

1Evaluate inside parentheses, brackets, or other grouping symbols.
2Evaluate exponents.
3Multiply and divide from left to right.
4Add and subtract from left to right.
Grouping Symbols

Parentheses can change the value

Without grouping8 + 4 × 3 = 8 + 12 = 20.
With grouping(8 + 4) × 3 = 12 × 3 = 36.
Fraction bars also groupTreat the numerator and denominator as grouped expressions when evaluating.
EXPONENTS & OPERATIONS

6 + 2³ × 4 = 6 + 8 × 4 = 38.

FRACTIONS & DECIMALS

The order of operations does not change just because the numbers are fractions or decimals.

Multiplication does not always come before division

Multiplication and division have equal priority, so work from left to right. Addition and subtraction work the same way.

Lesson 3-4: Write Algebraic Expressions

Algebraic expressions use variables to model patterns, word phrases, and real-world situations.

Translate Words to Math

Identify the operation and the unknown

5 more than nn + 5
5 less than nn − 5
6 times a number6n
A number divided by 4n/4
Parts of an Expression

Know what each part is doing

TermA number, variable, or product separated by addition or subtraction.
CoefficientThe numerical factor multiplying a variable. In 7x, 7 is the coefficient.
ConstantA term with no variable.
Factor & productFactors are quantities being multiplied; the product is the result of multiplication.
PATTERN

A comic book costs $4. If m is the number of books, the total cost is 4m.

REAL-WORLD MODEL

A service charges $12 plus $3 per hour. For h hours, the expression is 3h + 12.

Watch “less than” carefully

“5 less than n” means n − 5, not 5 − n. The wording reverses the order.

Lesson 3-5: Evaluate Algebraic Expressions

The goal is not just to “plug in a number.” Students must substitute accurately and then evaluate the resulting numerical expression in the correct order.

Core Procedure

Substitute → Order → Simplify

1Identify the value given for each variable.
2Substitute the value everywhere that variable appears. Parentheses can make the replacement easier to see.
3Use order of operations: exponents, multiplication/division, then addition/subtraction.
4Simplify until one numerical value remains. Check whether the answer makes sense in context.
Important Notation

A number beside a variable means multiply

3n = 3 × nIf n = 5, then 3n = 3(5) = 15.
ab = a × bIf a = 4 and b = 2.5, then ab = 4(2.5) = 10.
x² means x × xIf x = 3/2, then x² = (3/2)(3/2) = 9/4.
Common mistake: changing the order

For 8 + 3n when n = 4, substitute first: 8 + 3(4). Multiply before adding. The value is 20, not 44.

Common mistake: forgetting what the variable can represent

A variable can represent a whole number, decimal, or fraction. Real-world situations may place limits on reasonable values: a number of people should usually be a whole number, while a measurement may be fractional or decimal.

Lesson 3-6: Generate Equivalent Expressions

Equivalent expressions may look different, but they have the same value for every value that can be substituted for the variable.

Properties of Operations

Rewrite without changing the value

Commutative Propertya + b = b + a   and   ab = ba. You may change the order.
Associative Property(a + b) + c = a + (b + c). You may change the grouping.
Distributive Propertya(b + c) = ab + ac and a(b − c) = ab − ac.
Identity Propertiesa + 0 = a and a × 1 = a.
How to Justify Equivalence

Properties prove; substitution can test

1Look at the structure of both expressions.
2Use a property to rewrite one expression step by step.
3If both expressions rewrite to the same form, they are equivalent.
4To prove two expressions are not equivalent, one value that gives different results is enough.
DISTRIBUTE

4(2x + 3) = 8x + 12. Multiply 4 by both terms inside the parentheses.

FACTOR

12x + 6 = 6(2x + 1). The common factor 6 is divided out of both terms.

REORDER & REGROUP

3 + x + 7 = x + (3 + 7) = x + 10. Commutative and Associative Properties make the structure easier to see.

DISPROVE BY SUBSTITUTION

Compare 2(x + 4) and 2x + 4. If x = 3, the values are 14 and 10, so they are not equivalent.

Common mistake: distributing to only one term

In 5(x + 2), the 5 multiplies both x and 2. The equivalent expression is 5x + 10, not 5x + 2.

Lesson 3-7: Simplify Algebraic Expressions

Simplifying means writing an equivalent expression in a cleaner form. One major strategy is combining like terms.

Like Terms

Same variable part = like terms

Like terms2x and 7x are like terms. ½n and 3n are like terms. 5 and 12 are like terms because both are constants.
Not like terms2x and 2y are not like terms. x and x² are not like terms.
Combine coefficients3x + 7x = (3 + 7)x = 10x.
Simplifying Procedure

Organize, combine, check

1Use the Distributive Property first if parentheses need to be removed.
2Identify like terms by matching their variable parts.
3Use the Commutative/Associative Properties to place like terms together.
4Add or subtract the coefficients; combine constants separately.
BASIC

x + 5 + 2x + 2 = 3x + 7.

SUBTRACTION

9g − 2g + 4 = 7g + 4.

FRACTIONS

½n + ¾n + 2 = 1¼n + 2.

DECIMALS & DISTRIBUTION

2.5(x + 4) + 1.5x = 2.5x + 10 + 1.5x = 4x + 10.

Common mistake: combining unlike terms

You cannot combine 4x + 3 into 7x. The constant 3 does not contain x. You also cannot combine x with x².

a⁴ = a × a × a × a
G/L

GCF vs. LCM

GCF finds the greatest shared factor; LCM finds the least shared multiple.

GCF → equal groups   |   LCM → repeating events
OOP

Order of Operations

Grouping symbols, exponents, multiplication/division left to right, then addition/subtraction left to right.

( ) → exponents → × ÷ → + −
n

Write Algebraic Expressions

Choose a variable for the unknown, then translate the relationship into operations.

8 more than n → n + 8
≡

Equivalent Expressions

Different-looking expressions are equivalent when properties of operations show that they have the same value for every variable value.

3(x + 4) ≡ 3x + 12
D

Distributive Property

Multiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses. The property can also work in reverse to factor.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
LT

Like Terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part. Combine coefficients, not the variables themselves.

4x + 7x = 11x
≠

Not Like Terms

Different variables or different exponents cannot be combined.

3x + 2y stays 3x + 2y   |   x + x² cannot combine
S→O→S

Evaluate an Expression

Substitute the given value. Follow the Order of operations. Simplify until one value remains.

4 + 2x, x = 6 → 4 + 2(6) → 16
×

Invisible Multiplication

When a number and variable are written together, multiplication is understood.

5a = 5 × a    |    2xy = 2 × x × y
²

Exponents After Substitution

Replace the variable first, then evaluate the exponent before other multiplication, addition, or subtraction.

3r², r = 2 → 3(2²) → 3(4) → 12
½

Fractions & Decimals Work Too

The same procedure works when the variable value is not a whole number.

2.5m + 1, m = 1.2 → 2.5(1.2) + 1 → 4

Worked Examples

WHOLE NUMBER

Evaluate 20 + 3r when r = 14.
20 + 3(14) = 20 + 42 = 62

DECIMAL

A rental cost is represented by 8.75h + 4.50. Find the cost for h = 3.
8.75(3) + 4.50 = 26.25 + 4.50 = 30.75

FRACTION

Evaluate ¾p + 2 when p = 8.
¾(8) + 2 = 6 + 2 = 8

EXPONENT

Evaluate s² + ½ when s = 3/2.
(3/2)² + 1/2 = 9/4 + 2/4 = 11/4 = 2¾

TWO VARIABLES

Evaluate 5a + 2b when a = 3 and b = 4.
5(3) + 2(4) = 15 + 8 = 23

CHECK THE STRUCTURE

Evaluate 6 + 4x² when x = 2.
6 + 4(2²) = 6 + 16 = 22. Square before multiplying by 4.

Vocabulary

BaseThe repeated factor in a power.
ExponentThe number that tells how many times the base is used as a factor.
Prime factorizationA whole number written as a product of prime factors.
GCFThe greatest factor shared by two or more whole numbers.
LCMThe least positive multiple shared by two or more whole numbers.
Numerical expressionA mathematical phrase made of numbers and operations, with no variables.
VariableA letter or symbol used to represent a number.
Algebraic expressionA mathematical phrase containing numbers, operations, and one or more variables.
EvaluateFind the numerical value of an expression.
SubstituteReplace a variable with a specified numerical value.
CoefficientThe number multiplied by a variable. In 7x, the coefficient is 7.
Equivalent expressionsExpressions that have the same value for every allowed value of the variable.
TermA number, variable, or product of numbers and variables separated by addition or subtraction.
CoefficientThe numerical factor multiplying a variable.
ConstantA term with no variable.
Like termsTerms with the same variable part, including the same exponents.
