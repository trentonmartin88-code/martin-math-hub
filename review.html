How Do I Answer This Kind of Question?

Multiple ChoiceSolve before you shop. Work the problem first when possible, then find your result among the choices. If you are unsure, eliminate choices that cannot make sense.
Select All That ApplyTreat every option like its own True/False question. Do not stop after finding one correct answer.
Two Blanks / Drop-DownsSolve each blank independently. The first choice does not automatically tell you the second.
Yes / No TableCheck each row separately. Write a tiny calculation beside every statement before choosing Yes or No.
Number Line / Coordinate GridUse the picture as math evidence. On a number line, right is greater. On a coordinate plane, read x first, y second.
Explain Your ThinkingUse three parts: Answer → Evidence → Why. Give the answer, show the math/model, then explain why that evidence proves it.
Multi-Step Word ProblemRead the final question first. Identify the steps, solve in order, then ask: Did I answer what was actually asked?
When You Get StuckDo not freeze. Mark it, write what you know, eliminate anything impossible, move on, and come back with fresh eyes.

Answer Key + How to Think
1. C, 8. Convert 5 1/3 to 16/3, then divide by 2/3.
2. Greater. Dividing by 1/5 asks how many fifths fit into 3/4, so the quotient is larger than the dividend.
3. 115 R5. Check: 115×24 + 5 = 2,765.
4. A, $11.95. Add all purchases, then subtract from $40.
5. $140.125, or $140.13 to the nearest cent.
6. A. Distribute 6 to check: 42 + 30.
7. 18, |18|, and −(−18). Test each choice separately.
8. B. On a number line −0.35 is to the right of −0.7.
9. B. +20 and −20 are opposites and sum to zero.
10. −19 because |−19| = 19, the greatest distance listed.
11. III. B = (−6, −5). Both coordinates are negative.
12. A, (3, 7). Reflecting across the y-axis changes x only.
13. 12 units. The y-values match, so use the x-values: |5 − (−7)| = 12.
14. Missing vertex (−5, −3). Width 8, height 7, perimeter 30 units. Explain using shared x/y coordinates and absolute differences.
15. B. Zero describes the change, not the temperature itself.
16. Yes, Yes, No, Yes. Calculate each statement separately.
17. Pairs 1, 2, and 4. Pair 1 reflects across y; pairs 2 and 4 across x.
18. −9 is farther left than −4, so −9 < −4. The student compared absolute values instead of signed numbers.

Your Test-Day Plan

The Night Before

Prepare, Don't Cram

  • Spend 20–30 focused minutes on the skills you still miss.
  • Review your quick-reference rules and 2–3 sample problems.
  • Pack what you need for school.
  • Go to bed at a reasonable time. A rested brain works better than a midnight cram session.
The Morning Of

Start Steady

  • Eat something if you can.
  • Arrive prepared and avoid panic-reviewing everything at once.
  • Remind yourself of your strongest strategy: read, plan, solve, check.
  • If you feel nervous, slow your breathing and focus on one question at a time.
During the Test

Use the Whole Test

  • Read directions and identify the question type.
  • Estimate before computing when possible.
  • Use scratch work; do not try to hold every step in your head.
  • Skip and return instead of getting trapped on one item.
  • For select-all and tables, test each choice or row separately.
  • Before submitting, check that every required response is complete.
RememberYou do not have to know the whole test at once. You only have to solve the problem in front of you.
