Big Ideas

These are the ideas students should be able to explain—not just recognize.

➕

Signs Have Meaning

A positive or negative sign tells direction or position relative to a reference point. Always connect the sign to the situation.

0️⃣

Zero Is the Reference

Zero separates positive and negative values. Its meaning changes with context: sea level, no gain/loss, no debt, and more.

↔️

Position Shows Value

On a number line, values increase as you move right and decrease as you move left.

THE QUESTION TO ASK

“What does this number mean in the situation?”

Lessons 2-5 & 2-6

These notes follow the same skill progression students are using in class: find distance first, then use those distances to work with polygons.

Lesson 2-5 • Find Distances on the Coordinate Plane

Find horizontal and vertical distance using absolute value

When two points share an x-coordinate, the segment is vertical. When they share a y-coordinate, the segment is horizontal.

Vertical distance| y₂ − y₁ |The x-coordinates stay the same, so compare the y-values.
Horizontal distance| x₂ − x₁ |The y-coordinates stay the same, so compare the x-values.
Same side of zerosubtract absolute valuesExample: x = 8 and x = 3 → 8 − 3 = 5 units.
Opposite sides of zeroadd absolute valuesExample: y = −4 and y = 6 → 4 + 6 = 10 units.
Vertical exampleA(−3, 5) and B(−3, −4) share x = −3. Distance = |5 − (−4)| = 9 units.
Horizontal exampleC(−6, 2) and D(4, 2) share y = 2. Distance = |4 − (−6)| = 10 units.
Watch out for this:

Do not add or subtract the ordered pairs themselves. First identify whether the shared coordinate is x or y, then use the two coordinates that actually change.

Lesson 2-6 • Represent Polygons on the Coordinate Plane

Use coordinate distances to find side lengths and perimeter

1Plot or identify the vertices in order and connect the sides.
2Look for horizontal and vertical sides. Horizontal sides use x-values; vertical sides use y-values.
3Use absolute value to find each needed side length.
4Add all side lengths to find perimeter. For rectangles, opposite sides are equal, so you may not need to calculate every side separately.
Rectangle exampleVertices (−5, 4), (3, 4), (3, −2), (−5, −2) make a rectangle. Width = 8, height = 6, so perimeter = 8 + 6 + 8 + 6 = 28 units.
Missing vertex ideaFor an axis-aligned rectangle, the missing point combines an x-coordinate from one aligned vertex and a y-coordinate from the other aligned vertex.
Perimeter is not area.

Perimeter is the distance around the outside of a figure. Count or calculate side lengths, then add them. Do not multiply length × width unless the question asks for area.

Positive vs. Negative Numbers

Positive values are above, greater than, ahead, gained, deposited, or in the positive direction. Negative values are below, less than, behind, lost, withdrawn, or in the opposite direction.
Temperature8°F above zero → +8
5°F below zero → −5
MoneyDeposit $20 → +20
Owe $12 → −12
Elevation30 ft above sea level → +30
15 ft below sea level → −15
FootballGain 7 yards → +7
Loss 4 yards → −4

Worked Examples

Use these steps when you get stuck. The explanation matters as much as the final symbol or answer.

Example 1: Compare −10 and −3

Picture the number line.−10 is farther left than −3.
Use the comparison rule.The value farther left is less.
Write the comparison.−10 < −3

Example 2: Interpret −13 yards

Identify the reference.Zero represents no gain or loss.
Interpret the sign.The negative sign means movement in the losing direction.
State it in context.−13 represents a 13-yard loss.

Example 3: Which is farther from zero?

Compare −12 and 9.

Find each absolute value.|−12| = 12 and |9| = 9.
Compare the distances.12 is greater than 9.
Answer the question.−12 is farther from zero.

Example 4: Reflect across the y-axis

Point A is (4, 3).

Notice what stays the same.The y-coordinate stays 3.
Change the x-coordinate to its opposite.4 becomes −4.
Write the image point.(−4, 3)

Vocabulary

IntegerA whole number, its opposite, or zero. Examples: −4, 0, 9.
Positive NumberA number greater than zero.
Negative NumberA number less than zero.
OppositesNumbers the same distance from zero on opposite sides, such as −6 and 6.
Absolute ValueThe distance a number is from zero.
Rational NumberA number that can be written as a fraction of two integers, with a nonzero denominator.
Ordered PairTwo coordinates written (x, y) that name a point.
QuadrantOne of the four regions formed by the x-axis and y-axis.
