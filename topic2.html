Big Ideas

These are the ideas students should be able to explain—not just recognize.

➕

Signs Have Meaning

A positive or negative sign tells direction or position relative to a reference point. Always connect the sign to the situation.

0️⃣

Zero Is the Reference

Zero separates positive and negative values. Its meaning changes with context: sea level, no gain/loss, no debt, and more.

↔️

Position Shows Value

On a number line, values increase as you move right and decrease as you move left.

THE QUESTION TO ASK

“What does this number mean in the situation?”

Positive vs. Negative Numbers

Positive values are above, greater than, ahead, gained, deposited, or in the positive direction. Negative values are below, less than, behind, lost, withdrawn, or in the opposite direction.
Temperature8°F above zero → +8
5°F below zero → −5
MoneyDeposit $20 → +20
Owe $12 → −12
Elevation30 ft above sea level → +30
15 ft below sea level → −15
FootballGain 7 yards → +7
Loss 4 yards → −4

Absolute Value

Absolute value is the DISTANCE a number is from zero. Distance is never negative.
|−7| = 7−7 is 7 units from zero.
|5| = 55 is 5 units from zero.
Compare Distance|−12| = 12 and |9| = 9, so −12 is farther from zero.
ImportantAbsolute value tells distance—not which number is greater.

Worked Examples

Use these steps when you get stuck. The explanation matters as much as the final symbol or answer.

Example 1: Compare −10 and −3

Picture the number line.−10 is farther left than −3.
Use the comparison rule.The value farther left is less.
Write the comparison.−10 < −3

Example 2: Interpret −13 yards

Identify the reference.Zero represents no gain or loss.
Interpret the sign.The negative sign means movement in the losing direction.
State it in context.−13 represents a 13-yard loss.

Example 3: Which is farther from zero?

Compare −12 and 9.

Find each absolute value.|−12| = 12 and |9| = 9.
Compare the distances.12 is greater than 9.
Answer the question.−12 is farther from zero.

Example 4: Reflect across the y-axis

Point A is (4, 3).

Notice what stays the same.The y-coordinate stays 3.
Change the x-coordinate to its opposite.4 becomes −4.
Write the image point.(−4, 3)

Vocabulary

IntegerA whole number, its opposite, or zero. Examples: −4, 0, 9.
Positive NumberA number greater than zero.
Negative NumberA number less than zero.
OppositesNumbers the same distance from zero on opposite sides, such as −6 and 6.
Absolute ValueThe distance a number is from zero.
Rational NumberA number that can be written as a fraction of two integers, with a nonzero denominator.
Ordered PairTwo coordinates written (x, y) that name a point.
QuadrantOne of the four regions formed by the x-axis and y-axis.
